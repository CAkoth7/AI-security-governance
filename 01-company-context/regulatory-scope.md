# Regulatory and Framework Scope

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the regulatory and framework scope used for the Laurent Technologies AI System Inventory and Risk Classification project.

The purpose is to establish which regulatory and risk-management considerations will be incorporated into the project and to define the boundaries of the analysis.

---

## 2. Regulatory Reference Point

### EU Artificial Intelligence Act

The European Union Artificial Intelligence Act (EU AI Act) is used as a primary regulatory reference point for the classification of AI use cases in this project.

The project considers the AI Act's risk-based approach to AI systems, including the distinction between:

* Prohibited AI practices
* High-risk AI systems
* AI systems subject to transparency requirements
* Minimal or limited-risk use cases

The classification exercise will consider the characteristics and intended purpose of each AI system rather than assigning classifications solely based on the technology used.

Where classification is uncertain, the system will be recorded as requiring further legal or regulatory assessment rather than assigning a definitive classification.

---

## 3. NIST AI Risk Management Framework

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF) is used as the primary AI risk-management framework for this project.

The project applies the four NIST AI RMF functions:

### GOVERN

Establish organizational policies, accountability, roles, responsibilities, and governance mechanisms for AI risk management.

### MAP

Establish the context of an AI system, including its intended purpose, affected stakeholders, potential impacts, risks, assumptions, and limitations.

### MEASURE

Assess and monitor identified AI risks using appropriate qualitative, quantitative, or mixed methods.

### MANAGE

Prioritize identified risks and implement, document, and monitor appropriate risk responses.

---

## 4. Additional Governance Considerations

The project also considers broader governance areas relevant to an organization operating AI systems, including:

* Cybersecurity
* Data protection and privacy
* Data governance
* Third-party risk
* Model risk
* Operational resilience
* Human oversight
* AI system accountability
* AI lifecycle management
* Incident management
* Change management

These areas are considered from an enterprise risk and governance perspective rather than as a detailed legal compliance assessment.

---

## 5. Regulatory Classification vs Enterprise Risk

A key principle of this project is that regulatory classification should not be treated as equivalent to an organization's internal risk rating.

For example, an AI system that does not fall into a particular high-risk regulatory category could still present significant:

* Cybersecurity risk
* Privacy risk
* Operational risk
* Financial risk
* Reputational risk
* Data governance risk
* Third-party risk

The project will therefore maintain separate fields for:

**Regulatory Classification**

and

**Internal Enterprise AI Risk Rating**.

This distinction allows Laurent Technologies to consider both external regulatory requirements and its own risk appetite when determining appropriate governance measures.

---

## 6. Geographic Scope

Laurent Technologies is a fictional organization operating across European and selected international markets.

The project therefore uses the EU AI Act as an important regulatory reference point.

However, the project does not attempt to provide a comprehensive assessment of every national or international AI regulation that could potentially apply to Laurent Technologies.

Additional jurisdictions would require separate legal and regulatory analysis.

---

## 7. In Scope

The following activities are within the scope of this project:

* Identification of AI systems
* Creation of an AI system inventory
* Documentation of AI system ownership
* Documentation of AI system purpose and use
* Identification of data processed
* Identification of affected individuals
* Assessment of automation and human oversight
* EU AI Act-oriented classification
* Internal AI risk rating
* NIST AI RMF mapping
* Proportionate governance requirements
* AI lifecycle governance
* Review and monitoring requirements
* Third-party AI considerations
* Shadow AI considerations

---

## 8. Out of Scope

The following activities are outside the scope of this project:

* Formal legal advice
* Definitive legal interpretation of the EU AI Act
* Certification of regulatory compliance
* Detailed model validation
* Source-code security testing
* Penetration testing of AI systems
* Detailed privacy impact assessments
* Detailed algorithmic bias testing
* Full AI vendor due diligence
* Detailed financial model validation

These areas may be addressed in subsequent projects within the broader AI Security & Governance portfolio.

---

## 9. Classification Limitations

The AI classification exercise is a portfolio case study based on fictional systems and assumptions.

Actual regulatory classification may depend on detailed facts concerning:

* Intended purpose
* Deployment context
* System functionality
* Users
* Affected individuals
* Relationship between the AI system and human decision-making
* Applicable regulatory provisions and exceptions
* Jurisdiction

Where sufficient information is not available, the project will identify the classification as **Pending Further Assessment** rather than making an unsupported determination.

---
