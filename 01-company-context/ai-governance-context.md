# AI Governance Context

## 1. Background

Laurent Technologies is a fictional financial technology organization that is increasing its use of artificial intelligence across business and operational functions.

AI is being considered or deployed in areas including creditworthiness assessment, fraud detection, customer service, marketing personalization, recruitment, financial crime monitoring, document processing, and employee productivity.

As AI adoption increases, the organization needs a consistent approach to identifying, documenting, assessing, and governing its AI systems.

---

## 2. The Governance Problem

Without a centralized view of AI systems, Laurent Technologies may have limited visibility into:

* What AI systems are being used across the organization
* Who owns each AI system
* What business purpose each system serves
* What data each system processes
* Whether personal or sensitive information is involved
* Who may be affected by AI-supported decisions
* The degree of human oversight applied
* Which third-party providers are involved
* What regulatory requirements may apply
* What cybersecurity and operational risks exist
* How frequently each system should be reviewed

AI systems may also be introduced independently by different business units or employees, creating a risk of **unregistered or "shadow AI" use**.

---

## 3. Need for an AI System Inventory

Laurent Technologies therefore requires a structured AI system inventory that provides a centralized view of its AI landscape.

The inventory will capture information about each identified AI system, including:

* System identity
* Business purpose
* Business and technical ownership
* AI technology
* Data sources
* Personal and sensitive data usage
* Affected individuals
* Level of automation
* Human oversight
* Third-party dependencies
* Business impact
* Regulatory considerations
* Internal risk rating
* Lifecycle status
* Review requirements

The inventory will provide the foundation for subsequent AI risk assessment and governance activities.

---

## 4. Governance Objectives

The AI governance approach developed in this project aims to help Laurent Technologies:

### 4.1 Establish Visibility

Create a reliable and structured inventory of AI systems across the organization.

### 4.2 Establish Accountability

Assign clear business and technical ownership for AI systems.

### 4.3 Identify Risk

Identify regulatory, cybersecurity, privacy, operational, financial, and other risks associated with AI use.

### 4.4 Apply Proportionate Governance

Ensure that governance requirements are appropriate to the nature and level of risk presented by each AI system.

### 4.5 Support Regulatory Compliance

Provide a structured basis for considering applicable AI-related regulatory requirements.

### 4.6 Enable Continuous Oversight

Establish review and monitoring mechanisms throughout the AI system lifecycle.

---

## 5. Governance Principles

The project adopts the following principles:

### Accountability

Every AI system should have an identifiable owner responsible for its appropriate use and ongoing oversight.

### Transparency

The organization should maintain sufficient information about AI systems to understand their purpose, operation, limitations, and potential impacts.

### Risk-Based Governance

Governance requirements should be proportionate to the risks associated with an AI system.

### Human Oversight

AI-supported decisions should have appropriate human oversight based on the nature and consequences of the use case.

### Security by Design

Cybersecurity considerations should be incorporated throughout the AI lifecycle rather than being addressed only after deployment.

### Data Governance

AI systems should use appropriate, reliable, and properly governed data for their intended purposes.

### Continuous Monitoring

AI risks should be reassessed as systems, models, data, threats, regulations, and business use cases change.

---

## 6. AI Lifecycle Consideration

The governance approach considers AI systems throughout their lifecycle:

```text
Idea / Proposal
      ↓
Assessment
      ↓
Development / Procurement
      ↓
Testing
      ↓
Approval
      ↓
Deployment
      ↓
Monitoring
      ↓
Change / Reassessment
      ↓
Retirement
```

Governance requirements may change depending on the lifecycle stage and risk profile of the AI system.

---

## 7. Shadow AI

The project also considers the possibility that employees may use AI services that have not been formally approved or recorded by the organization.

Examples could include employees using publicly available generative AI tools to:

* Summarize internal documents
* Analyze business information
* Draft customer communications
* Generate code
* Process operational information

Such use may introduce risks involving data leakage, confidentiality, intellectual property, privacy, security, and regulatory compliance.

The AI inventory should therefore support identification and management of both formally approved AI systems and potentially unregistered AI use.

---

## 8. Governance Outcome

The intended outcome is a structured AI governance foundation that allows Laurent Technologies to move from:

**Limited visibility**

to

**AI inventory**

to

**Risk classification**

to

**Enterprise risk assessment**

to

**Proportionate governance**

to

**Continuous monitoring and review**.

This project will use that progression as the basis for the subsequent AI inventory, classification, risk assessment, and governance activities.
